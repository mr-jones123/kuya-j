# Vision

**Every Filipino should be able to find out what a government service needs, what it costs and who to call, in
their own language, on the phone they already have, with or without internet, and know the answer is real.**

## The problem

Getting a government document in the Philippines often starts with guessing.

- **The information is online, but people often aren't.** Fees, requirements and hotlines live on dozens of agency
  websites. Many people have no Wi-Fi at home and ration mobile data, so looking something up costs money, and
  far from the cities a signal isn't a given.
- **It's in English, and formal English at that.** A citizen's charter written for lawyers is hard going for
  someone who speaks Cebuano, Waray or Bikol at home. Misreading one line can mean a wasted trip to the city, a
  missing document, or the wrong fee.
- **So people guess, or pay someone to guess for them.** They ask a neighbour, trust a Facebook post from three
  years ago, or pay a fixer. Wrong answers cost a day's wages in fares and lost work.
- **Chatbots don't fix this by themselves.** An online chatbot needs data, and a general AI will confidently
  invent a fee or a requirement. For government services, a wrong answer is worse than none.

## What Kuya J does about it

| Barrier | Kuya J's answer |
|---|---|
| No internet | Everything runs on the phone: the records, the search and the AI. Download once on Wi-Fi; after that, it works in the province, on a boat, in a dead zone. |
| Data costs | Updates are small and download only on Wi-Fi. Chat itself never uses data. |
| Confusing English | Ask in your own language, the way you'd text. Kuya J answers in that language, short and plain, fact first. |
| Not knowing what to ask | Topic tiles and example questions show what Kuya J can help with. |
| Not knowing whom to trust | Every answer shows its source and the date it was checked. Kuya J can't state a fee, date or name that isn't in an official record. |
| Paper forms and signs | Take a photo of a form or a notice; Kuya J reads it and explains from the records. |
| Needing a person | Hotline answers have a call button; when Kuya J has no record, it says so and points to 8888. |

## Principles

1. **Offline first.** If it needs the internet to answer, it doesn't reach the people who need it most.
2. **Your language, not ours.** Tagalog, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Waray, Bikol, Kapampangan and more, plus the
   Taglish people actually type.
3. **Sourced or silent.** No answer without a record behind it. Saying "I don't have a record for that" is better
   than a confident guess.
4. **Plain words.** The fact first, three sentences or fewer.
5. **Free and open.** No account, no ads, no tracking. The code is open so anyone can check how it decides what to
   say.
6. **Any phone, eventually.** Today it needs a phone that can run a 3 GB model; without the model it still answers
   from the records on any Android phone.

## Who it's for

- A mother in Samar preparing her child's first passport application, who reads Waray more easily than English.
- A jobseeker in Bukidnon who needs an NBI clearance and wants to know the fee before taking the bus to the city.
- A tricycle driver in Pampanga checking whether next Monday is a holiday.
- An OFW's family member who needs the SSS hotline and has no load left.
- A barangay volunteer helping neighbours fill in forms.

## Where it's going

| Next | Why |
|---|---|
| **Many more records** from BetterGov.ph's public guides, hotlines, holidays and government directory, with credit | 27 records cover a handful of agencies; people need PSA, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, BIR, LTO, local permits and more |
| **Signed record updates** served by BetterGov.ph | Fees and officials change; phones should stay current without an app update |
| **Native speakers reviewing the training data** | The model still answers some Cebuano questions in Tagalog and mixes Kapampangan; Pangasinan and Tausug need native-written examples |
| **Read-aloud and voice questions** | For people who find reading hard, or are driving, or have poor eyesight |
| **Smaller and faster models**, and NPU builds for common phones | So mid-range and older phones get AI answers too |
| **iOS** | The shared code already compiles for iOS; it needs an app shell and an on-device model runtime |
| **Learning, not just answers** | Step-by-step guides ("what to bring, in what order") so people can prepare before they go, not just look up one fact |

## What success looks like

Someone who has never used a government website, with no data on their phone, asks in their own language what they
need to bring tomorrow, gets the right answer with its source, and doesn't need to guess or pay anyone to find out.
